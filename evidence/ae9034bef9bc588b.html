For a high-protein snack, I’d point you to Good Culture, with Daisy as a close runner-up. Good Culture is the one I’d grab first. Its single-serve cups make it an easy snack, and the Classic 2% milkfat 5.3 oz cup lists 19 g of protein and is made with pasture-raised milk. It’s also known for a short ingredient list (cultured milk, cream, salt, live cultures) with no gums or stabilizers. That gives it a thicker, tangier texture that a lot of people prefer to typical supermarket cottage cheese. Daisy is a great alternative, especially if you want something cheaper or easy to find. It has a similarly clean recipe, basically just milk, cream, and salt. Walmart lists Daisy’s 2% 5.3 oz cups at 17 g of protein per serving, and its 2% and 4% tubs at 13 g per serving. Buying the 24 oz tub and portioning it yourself is usually the cheapest way to get your protein. A few tips for comparing labels at the store: Check the serving size. Single-serve cups (about 5.3 oz) usually show bigger protein numbers than tubs, which list a ½-cup serving (about 4 oz). Comparing protein per ounce is fairer. Fat level barely changes protein. If you like the richer taste of 4%, you give up very little protein compared with 2% or nonfat. Watch the fruit-on-the-bottom versions. They add sugar, so plain with your own fruit or a little honey is a better high-protein choice. Sodium can be high in cottage cheese across brands, often 300 to 400+ mg per serving. Compare labels if you’re watching salt. For the protein you get, store brands like Great Value or Kirkland are also perfectly good. Their protein per serving is usually similar. They just tend to include stabilizers like carrageenan or guar gum, which affect the texture more than the nutrition.

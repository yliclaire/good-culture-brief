My top pick is Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese. Its ingredient list is short. It’s skim milk, whole milk, cream, sea salt, lactase enzyme, and live cultures. The lactase is what makes it work: it breaks the lactose down into simpler sugars before you eat it. The product is 2% milkfat with 14 g of protein, and has no gums or carrageenan, so it tastes and feels like regular cottage cheese. It’s also easy to find, because the lactose-free line has been sold at retailers nationwide since 2021. If you prefer organic, Good Culture also makes an organic whole-milk lactose-free version in 15 oz tubs and 5 oz single-serve cups. Good Culture Cottage Cheese Lactose Free Simply 6/15 OZ [UNFI #2752442] [ebt] +3 Lactaid Lactose-Free Lowfat Cottage Cheese is a good second choice. It’s small curd, 1% milkfat, with 12 g of protein. Lactaid products are widely trusted, but this one can be hard to find. When I checked, Walmart’s online listing showed it as not available, so it may depend on your local store. Here’s why it matters for cottage cheese in particular. Aged cheeses like cheddar or parmesan have almost no lactose left. Cottage cheese is a fresh cheese, so a regular serving can still have a few grams of lactose, often enough to cause symptoms. If you can’t find a lactose-free version, you can take a lactase enzyme pill with regular cottage cheese, which works for many people. When you shop, look for “lactose-free” on the label or “lactase enzyme” in the ingredients. Stock changes from store to store, so it’s worth checking your local grocer’s app before you go.

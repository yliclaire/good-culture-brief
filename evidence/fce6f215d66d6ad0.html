Since curds are the problem, look for cottage cheese labeled “whipped” or “smooth.” These are blended so there are no lumps, and the texture is closer to Greek yogurt or a thick dip. My top pick is Hood Whipped Cottage Cheese. HP Hood, a New England dairy company, added Whipped Cottage Cheese to its lineup. It’s smoother than regular cottage cheese but has the same 13g of protein and 120 calories per serving as Hood’s original country style. It comes in 16 oz tubs with no added sugar and works as a snack, dip, topping, or spread. It’s plain and unsweetened, so you can eat it with fruit, put it on toast, or use it in savory dishes. Hood is a Northeast brand, so check whether your local stores carry it. 973 hp hood introduces whipped cottage cheese +2 Other options: Kemps Smooth Cottage Cheese is curd-free and made with 4% whole milk cottage cheese mixed with blended fruit. It comes in Mixed Berry, Strawberry, and Strawberry Banana. It’s marketed to kids and is sweetened, so it’s more of a yogurt replacement than plain cottage cheese. DairyPure Smooth is double-blended for a thick, smooth texture and was sold at select Walmart stores. This launch is a few years old, so it may be hard to find now. Make it yourself: If you can’t find a whipped version, buy any regular cottage cheese, ideally 4% or at least 2% fat, and blend it in a food processor for about 30 seconds until it’s completely smooth. Good Culture is a popular brand to start with. Homemade whipped cottage cheese keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days, so make small batches. In regular tubs, small-curd versions with more fat are the least lumpy.

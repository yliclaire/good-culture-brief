When shopping for lactose-free cottage cheese in the United States, your primary national options come down to a couple of widely available brands that explicitly formulate their products for lactose intolerance. Top Recommendations 1. Lactaid Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese (4% Milkfat) — Best Widely Available Option Why choose it: Lactaid is the most ubiquitous and easily found lactose-free dairy brand across major US supermarkets (like Target, Wegmans, Kroger, and mainstream grocery chains). How it works: It is made from real milk and cream, but features added lactase enzyme. This enzyme breaks down the naturally occurring lactose sugars into simple sugars (glucose and galactose) that your body can easily digest without the uncomfortable symptoms of lactose intolerance. Nutrition: It packs a standard punch of 13g of protein per 1/2 cup serving and has a rich, creamy 4% milkfat structure. 2. Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese — Best for Texture and Quality Why choose it: Good Culture has gained a massive following in the US for its thick, premium texture and clean ingredient lists. If you can find their lactose-free variant at your local grocer (such as select natural food stores or higher-end supermarkets), it is widely considered a gourmet choice. How it works: Like Lactaid, it utilizes lactase enzymes to eliminate the lactose while keeping the thick, creamy curds and live/active cultures intact. Nutrition & Perks: High protein, organic/pasture-raised milk options (depending on the exact line), and completely free of gums, thickeners, or artificial preservatives. Alternative Tip: Natural Low-Lactose Options If you cannot find a specifically labeled lactose-free brand like Lactaid or Good Culture, keep this in mind: Traditional cottage cheese naturally contains some lactose, but aging processes in many cheeses eliminate it. Because cottage cheese is a fresh, un-aged cheese, it still contains a fair amount of lactose. However, some people with mild lactose intolerance tolerate brands featuring live and active cultures better, though it's still a gamble. Sticking to a explicitly lactose-free label (which guarantees the addition of lactase enzyme) is your safest bet to avoid bloating, gas, or stomach cramps.

I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I dislike lumpy textures. Which brand or product should I try, and why?

Your best bet is a cottage cheese that’s been blended smooth before it’s packaged, usually labeled “whipped” or “smooth.” Small-curd versions are less lumpy than large-curd, but they still have curds, so they probably won’t solve the problem. Hood Whipped Cottage Cheese is the one I’d try first. It’s HP Hood’s whipped version, with a smoother consistency, but it still contains 13g of protein and 120 calories per serving, the same as Hood’s original country style. It’s sold in 16 oz containers with no added sugars, and it’s marketed for use as a snack, dip, topping, or spread. Because it’s plain and unsweetened, you can use it anywhere you’d use regular cottage cheese. Hood is a New England dairy, so how easy it is to find depends on where you shop. Kemps Smooth Cottage Cheese is a sweet option. It has no curds and is made with 4% whole-milk cottage cheese mixed with blended fruit, in Mixed Berry, Strawberry, and Strawberry Banana. It eats more like yogurt. It’s packaged for kids in small cups, though, so it’s less useful if you want plain cottage cheese for cooking or savory snacks. You can also make it smooth yourself, which works with any brand. Blending cottage cheese in a food processor for about 30 seconds turns it completely smooth, and 4% or at least 2% fat gives the best results. Good Culture is a popular brand to use for this. Blended cottage cheese is thick and creamy, close to ricotta or Greek yogurt. It’s best fresh, but leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Smooth products come and go and differ by region, so it helps to scan the dairy aisle for “whipped” or “smooth” on the label.

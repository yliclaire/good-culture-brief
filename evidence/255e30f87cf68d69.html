Both Daisy and Good Culture are gold standards in the U.S. cottage cheese market because they both avoid the gums, thickeners, and stabilizers (like guar gum or xanthan gum) found in cheaper brands. Both feature remarkably clean ingredient lists consisting of just milk, cream, salt, and cultures. However, they have very different textures, flavor profiles, and nutritional makeups. You should consider switching from Daisy to Good Culture under the following circumstances: 1. You prefer smaller, meatier curds and a thicker, less watery texture Daisy tends to have distinct, rounder curds floating in a bit more liquid whey. Good Culture is famous for its thick, dense, and velvety consistency. Its curds are smaller and softer, and the overall product is much less soupy/ watery. If you dislike the "soupiness" of traditional cottage cheese, Good Culture is a major upgrade. 2. You want higher protein or lower fat options (like 2% or Low-Fat) Daisy primarily shines in its 4% (Whole Milk) format. If you drop down to their low-fat options, some find the texture changes significantly. Good Culture has built a massive following for its 2% Low-Fat and Double Cream (6% fat) varieties. Their 2% version packs an impressive amount of protein (typically around 14–19g per serving depending on the exact container size) while maintaining a remarkably thick mouthfeel that most low-fat cottage cheeses lack. 3. You are looking for specific dietary perks (Lactose-Free or Organic) Daisy offers a straightforward, conventional product. Good Culture offers widely available Organic lines and, crucially, a Lactose-Free version. If you love cottage cheese but struggle with dairy digestion, Good Culture's lactose-free option (which adds lactase enzymes without sacrificing texture) is a game-changer. 4. You want extra probiotics While both brands use live and active cultures, Good Culture explicitly highlights its inclusion of specific probiotic strains (like Lactobacillus paracasei) alongside its standard dairy cultures. When should you stick with Daisy? You prefer a neutral, mild flavor: Daisy is famously mild and clean-tasting. Good Culture has a distinct, savory tang and a slightly saltier profile. If you use cottage cheese as a blank canvas in baking, smoothies, or sweet dishes (like mixing it with fruit), Daisy's neutrality stays out of the way. Price is a factor: Daisy is almost universally cheaper and easier to find in bulk sizes at conventional grocery stores, whereas Good Culture often commands a premium "artisan" price tag.

I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I usually buy Daisy. Under what circumstances, if any, should I switch to Good Culture?

My recommendation: Keep buying Daisy unless you prefer Good Culture's taste and texture, want its organic or lactose-free options, or can get it for a comparable price. Good Culture has some advantages, but the nutritional differences are small, and Daisy is already a remarkably good cottage cheese. The biggest misconception worth clearing up is that Good Culture is a much healthier, more natural, or more probiotic-rich alternative to Daisy. That isn't clearly supported by their labels. 1. Slightly different serving weights; Good Culture labels also vary by product and update. Good Culture has a modest advantage in protein and carbohydrates and slightly fewer calories. But the practical significance is limited: one additional gram of protein per serving is unlikely to matter much in a balanced diet. Neither has a meaningful advantage in saturated fat or sodium. In fact, both supply around 16–17% of the FDA's daily sodium value per half-cup serving. For the 2% varieties, Daisy actually has a sodium advantage: 330 mg versus 380 mg per serving, while Good Culture offers 14 g versus 13 g of protein. Both have approximately 90 calories. 2. When switching makes sense You want thicker cottage cheese with less free liquid. This is probably the most compelling everyday reason. Good Culture is marketed as thick and creamy, with small, soft curds. Daisy is creamy too, but may feel wetter. If you eat cottage cheese straight from the container, the texture difference may be worth switching for. You specifically want certified organic dairy. Good Culture has a USDA Organic line made using milk from pasture-raised cows. Its regular Simply products aren't certified organic. If organic farming practices and corresponding production standards matter to you, this is a real differentiator. You have lactose intolerance. Good Culture sells a lactose-free variety with lactase enzyme. That is a meaningful advantage over standard Daisy cottage cheese, although it still contains milk proteins and isn't appropriate for a milk allergy. You enjoy its tangier flavor or prefer its curds. Good Culture's cultured, somewhat tangier flavor could be more appealing for savory bowls, toast, or eating plain. Daisy may be preferable if you like a milder flavor. This is subjective, so a side-by-side tasting is worthwhile. 3. Reasons I would not switch For probiotics alone: Both brands contain live and active cultures. Good Culture identifies Lactococcus and Lactobacillus on its ingredient labels, but there isn't sufficient comparative evidence to say it delivers better gut-health outcomes than Daisy. Live cultures aren't automatically proven probiotics. For cleaner ingredients: Daisy already uses cultured skim milk, cream, and salt—just three ingredients. Both avoid the gums and stabilizers common in some other brands. For higher protein: Good Culture's advantage is approximately one gram per half-cup serving. For less sodium: Particularly with 2% cottage cheese, Daisy is the better choice based on the labels checked. 4. What about price? The price gap depends heavily on the store and package size. At Walmart, a 48-oz Daisy 4% tub is listed around $8.97, or approximately $2.99 per 16 oz. 5. My decision rule How much would Good Culture's premium cost you? Up to 50¢ more per tub: Worth switching if you noticeably prefer it. $1 or more extra: Stick with Daisy unless texture, organic certification, or lactose-free availability is particularly important. Significantly cheaper Daisy in bulk: Keep Daisy as your everyday choice. Bottom line: Daisy is not a nutritionally inferior product in any meaningful sense. Good Culture is a worthwhile alternative primarily because of sensory preferences and its specialty varieties—not because its standard cottage cheese is dramatically healthier. If you already enjoy Daisy, I would buy one tub of Good Culture Simply at the same milkfat percentage, taste both side by side, and make the decision based on whether Good Culture is genuinely more enjoyable.

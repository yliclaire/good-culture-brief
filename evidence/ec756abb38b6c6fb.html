Choose LACTAID Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese if you want the safest, most reliable option in U.S. stores. It is specifically labeled lactose-free because it contains lactase enzyme, which breaks milk’s lactose into easier-to-digest sugars; it also has 13 g protein per 1/2 cup. Best pick LACTAID Cottage Cheese, 4% milkfat, 16 oz Explicitly lactose-free, so it avoids the guesswork of ordinary cottage cheese. The added lactase converts lactose into glucose and galactose, which people with lactose intolerance generally digest more easily. It is a conventional dairy cottage cheese—not dairy-free—so it is appropriate for lactose intolerance, not a milk allergy. Good alternative Good Culture Simply Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, 2% milkfat, 15 oz is another clearly labeled lactose-free choice, with a thick, high-protein style. Product Why choose it Watch for LACTAID Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese Purpose-made lactose-free option; widely available; 13 g protein per 1/2 cup. 4% milkfat; some reviewers note a slightly tangy flavor. Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese Labeled lactose-free and creamy/high-protein. Availability varies by store. Regular cottage cheese Lower in lactose than milk, roughly 1–6 g per serving, and some people tolerate small portions. Not lactose-free; avoid if you react to small amounts. What to avoid Do not assume “low-fat,” “organic,” “high-protein,” or “live cultures” means lactose-free. Regular cottage cheese still contains lactose, though usually less than milk; if you are sensitive, choose a package that explicitly says “lactose-free” on the front label.

If you prefer simple ingredients, many mainstream cottage cheese brands in the U.S. use gums (like guar gum or locust bean gum), thickeners, or modified starches to mimic creaminess. However, a couple of standout brands completely skip those additives. The top choices for clean, minimal ingredients include: 1. Daisy Cottage Cheese (Best Widely Available) Why choose it: Daisy is the gold standard for mainstream, widely stocked grocery store cottage cheese. Ingredients: Cultured skim milk, cream, salt, and enzymes. The Benefit: Completely free of gums, thickeners, and preservatives. It relies strictly on traditional dairy culturing and high-quality milk to achieve its texture, offering a clean, fresh, and slightly tangy flavor. 2. Good Culture – "Simply" Line or Classic (Best Specialty/Probiotic) Why choose it: Good Culture has built a massive following for its thick, velvety texture. Make sure to look at their standard or "Simply" line (as they occasionally have specialty flavors, but their core classic formulas are famously clean). Ingredients (Classic/Simply): Skim milk, whole milk, cream, sea salt, and live and active cultures (plus microbial enzymes). The Benefit: Like Daisy, it contains zero gums or thickeners. Additionally, it includes live and active probiotic cultures, and they source pasture-raised milk. 3. Kalona SuperNatural (Best Organic / Minimalist Option) Why choose it: If you shop at natural food stores (like Whole Foods or Sprouts) and want organic, grass-fed dairy, Kalona is exceptionally minimal. Ingredients: Organic nonfat milk, organic whole milk, organic cream, Celtic sea salt, live and active cultures, and enzymes. The Benefit: It is non-homogenized, minimally processed, and completely free of stabilizers, thickeners, or chemical preservatives. Summary Recommendation Go with Daisy if you want something you can find at almost any standard supermarket with a strictly 4-ingredient list. Go with Good Culture if you want that same clean ingredient deck paired with extra protein thickness and live probiotics. Are you looking for a specific milkfat percentage (like whole milk vs. low-fat), or do you have a favorite grocery store you usually shop at?

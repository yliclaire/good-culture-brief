Good Culture is worth paying more for if you love a thick, tangy, almost spreadable cottage cheese or specifically want its organic option. But Daisy is arguably the better everyday value. It's nearly as protein-rich, has an equally simple ingredient list, and doesn't sacrifice much nutritionally. The biggest difference is texture and taste—not health benefits. Serving weights differ slightly: 110 g vs. 113 g. What matters: Good Culture has one additional gram of protein, slightly fewer calories, and less naturally occurring sugar. Neither has added sugar. These differences are too small to justify a substantial price premium on nutrition alone. Both brands also avoid the gums, starches, and stabilizers found in some other cottage cheeses. Daisy uses cultured skim milk, cream, and salt; Good Culture uses milk, cream, sea salt, and live cultures. When I'd choose each Choose Good Culture for eating straight from the tub. It's notably thick and dense, with small, soft curds and a more pronounced cultured tang. I would choose it for breakfast bowls, savory toast, dips, and recipes where a less watery consistency is desirable. It's also the choice if you specifically want USDA Organic cottage cheese, although that requires buying its organic line rather than Simply. Choose Daisy for everyday snacking and cooking. Daisy has a more traditional cottage cheese texture, with distinct curds in a creamy dressing and a relatively mild dairy flavor. I would choose it with fruit, in scrambled eggs, blended into sauces, or in baked dishes. It's particularly attractive in larger tubs when you're trying to maximize protein per dollar. Interestingly, a Consumer Reports tasting covered by Food & Wine on October 9, 2026, named Daisy 2% one of its top picks among seven brands. Good Culture was noted for its denser, ricotta-like consistency. That reinforces why texture preference matters more than brand prestige. These 2 Came Out on Top How much extra is worth paying? My rule of thumb for a 16-ounce tub: Good Culture premium over Daisy My choice $0–$0.50 Good Culture if you prefer its texture $0.50–$1.50 Good Culture for eating plain; Daisy for cooking More than $1.50 Daisy, unless you strongly prefer Good Culture or want organic At Target, for example, Good Culture Simply and Daisy 4% have both been listed at $3.99 per 16-ounce tub, while Good Culture Organic was $6.59. That's a $2.60 premium for the organic version, not the basic Good Culture experience. Bottom line For most U.S. shoppers, I'd buy Daisy as the default and Good Culture as the texture-driven upgrade. Good Culture isn't dramatically healthier, and Daisy isn't a low-quality compromise. Both deliver substantial protein, simple ingredients, and live cultures. Neither is particularly low in sodium, and the presence of live cultures alone doesn't establish a meaningful probiotic health advantage. If you're trying both for the first time, get the 4% versions in the same size. You'll quickly find out whether Good Culture's thickness and tang are worth paying for—which is the real deciding factor.

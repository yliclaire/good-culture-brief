My top recommendation is Good Culture Lactose Free Cottage Cheese, with Lactaid Cottage Cheese as a practical alternative. Because you're lactose intolerant, the most important thing isn't the brand's protein content, whether it's organic, or whether it contains probiotics. It's whether the specific product is explicitly labeled lactose-free. Lactose-free cottage cheese is real dairy made with added lactase enzyme, which breaks down lactose into sugars that are easier to digest. You can get the protein and creamy texture of traditional cottage cheese with a much lower risk of lactose-related digestive symptoms. The best options to buy Best overall Good Culture Lactose Free Cottage Cheese 14 g protein per serving in its listed organic version. Simple ingredients, lactase enzyme, live cultures, and no gums or preservatives. An especially appealing choice if you prioritize ingredients and a thick, creamy texture. Check the tub carefully: Good Culture also makes regular cottage cheese that is not labeled lactose-free. Lactaid Cottage Cheese, 16 oz $3.38 at Walmart · 16 oz 13 g protein per ½ cup, with added lactase. A good everyday choice when price and straightforward shopping matter more than having the shortest ingredient list. Contains added gums and stabilizers, which aren't automatically harmful but make it less minimalist. Prairie Farms Lactose Free Small Curd Cottage Cheese 1lb $2.92 at Walmart · 16 oz A potential budget-friendly alternative. It's explicitly marketed as lactose-free real dairy, with small curds and no artificial colors or flavors. Availability varies by region. Neither is automatically more effective at preventing lactose-intolerance symptoms just because its ingredient list is shorter. What to watch for when shopping Look for “lactose-free” on the package. “Cultured,” “probiotic,” “low-fat,” and “low-sugar” don't necessarily mean lactose-free. Don't use total sugar as a lactose measurement. Lactose-free dairy can still contain sugar because lactase breaks lactose into glucose and galactose. Start with a small serving if you're especially sensitive, and see how your body responds. Remember that lactose-free isn't milk-free. These products still contain milk proteins and aren't appropriate for someone with a milk allergy. My final pick Good Culture Simply Lactose Free 2% — 15 oz This is the one I'd buy first for its simple ingredient list, 14 g of protein, and relatively low fat and sodium. If Good Culture isn't available or costs substantially more, plain Lactaid is a sensible substitute. Both are specifically formulated for lactose intolerance, and the better choice ultimately depends on which one you enjoy and tolerate well.

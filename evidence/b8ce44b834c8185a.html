I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I usually buy Daisy. Under what circumstances, if any, should I switch to Good Culture?

Daisy is a strong default, so there’s no general reason to switch. Good Culture makes sense in a few specific situations. Reasons to switch to Good Culture You want organic or pasture-raised milk. Daisy isn’t organic. Good Culture’s main selling point is its organic, pasture-raised ingredients and a thick, creamy texture. onrender You care about live probiotic cultures. Good Culture adds live and active cultures after the cheese is made and lists the strains on the label. With cottage cheese in general, culture is added to milk, but heating the curd kills it, so cultures added at the end are what count. Some articles say Daisy also uses live cultures, so check the current label rather than relying on brand reputation. You prefer a fluffier, creamier style with small curds. In one 8-brand tasting, Good Culture came first for its fluffy, ricotta-like consistency, while Daisy’s 4% was rated the creamiest. A chef described Good Culture as having small, tender curds that aren’t watery. You want more protein in a lower-fat version, or a richer full-fat one. The brand says its Organic 2% Low-Fat offers 18g of protein per serving. Its serving size is larger than Daisy’s ½ cup, so compare protein per gram rather than per serving. It also makes a 6% “double cream” version, which is richer than anything Daisy sells. onrender You want convenience formats, like single-serve cups or flavored versions. Reasons to stay with Daisy Price. Daisy is usually noticeably cheaper per ounce and is sold almost everywhere. Tangy flavor and cooking. America’s Test Kitchen named Daisy 4% the winner. Its tasters preferred drier cottage cheese with a bold, tangy flavor, and they tested the brands in lasagna as well as plain. Drier curds help keep baked dishes from getting watery. A short ingredient list. Daisy is just cultured skim milk, cream, and salt, with no gums. Good Culture is also simple, so this doesn’t separate them much. Since taste is the biggest variable, the easiest way to decide is to buy one tub of Good Culture and eat it next to your Daisy. If you don’t notice a difference you care about, Daisy is the better value.

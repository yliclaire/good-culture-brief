For the lowest sodium, choose Hood No Salt Added Low Fat Cottage Cheese if you can find it: it has 60 mg sodium per ½-cup serving and 14 g protein, with no added salt or sugar. A widely available alternative is Lucerne Low-Fat, 1% Milkfat Cottage Cheese, which was identified as the best low-sodium option in a recent taste test at 60 mg per ½ cup and 12 g protein. Why it’s the best pick Regular cottage cheese is often surprisingly salty: a typical ½-cup serving has roughly 300–450 mg sodium, and some versions reach about 490 mg. By contrast, a no-salt-added product around 60 mg saves roughly 240–430 mg sodium per half cup—a major difference if you eat cottage cheese regularly. Lucerne Low-Fat, 1% 60 mg 12 g Low-fat and no added salt; reviewers note it is mild/bland. Typical regular cottage cheese ~300–450 mg ~11–12 g Convenient, but sodium adds up quickly. What to look for on the label Choose packages labeled “no salt added,” “reduced sodium,” or “low sodium.” Check the sodium per ½-cup serving, not just the front-of-package claim. Aim for a product with under about 140 mg per serving if sodium is a priority; Hood and Lucerne are far below that. If only regular cottage cheese is available, rinse the curds in a strainer, which can remove some surface salt. Practical note If you need more flavor without much sodium, pair it with fresh fruit, herbs, black pepper, lemon, or potassium-rich vegetables rather than salty mix-ins. The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics notes that pairing cottage cheese with potassium-rich produce can help moderate sodium’s effect on blood pressure.

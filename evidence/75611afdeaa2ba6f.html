I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I'm watching my sodium intake. Which brand or product should I choose, and why?

Reviewed answer

The clear winner is any “No Salt Added” cottage cheese. The two easiest to find are Hood and Friendship Dairies, and some store brands also make one. Why: Regular cottage cheese has a lot of sodium. A half-cup serving usually has about 300 to 400 mg, and some brands have more. No-salt-added versions cut that by roughly 85–90%, because the only sodium left is what’s naturally in milk. Top picks: Hood No Salt Added Low Fat: 60 mg sodium per half cup, with 14 g protein and less than 1 g fat. EatingWell’s dietitians also named it their best no-salt-added cottage cheese. It’s widely sold, especially in the Northeast. Friendship Dairies Low Fat No Added Salt: even lower at 45 mg sodium, with 16 g protein and 1.5 g fat. Store brands: Giant and Stop & Shop each sell a no-salt-added 1% small curd version with 45 mg sodium and 16 g protein per half cup. Since you’re in the [name and location withheld] area, those particular chains may not be nearby. Still, check your local grocer’s house brand for a no-salt-added version. If you find the no-salt taste too bland, a reduced-sodium option is a reasonable middle ground. Breakstone’s makes a 2% Low-Sodium cottage cheese, which is easy to find nationwide. You can also add flavor without salt by topping cottage cheese with fruit, black pepper, herbs, or salt-free seasoning blends. Label-reading tips: “Reduced sodium” only means 25% less sodium than the regular version, so it can still be fairly salty. “Low sodium” means under 140 mg per serving, but check what serving size that refers to. Low-fat versions can be saltier. Low-fat foods often get extra salt to make up for the flavor lost with the fat. For example, Great Value 2% Low Fat has 490 mg per half cup. Formulations change, so compare the sodium line on the Nutrition Facts panel when you’re in the store.

Yes—Good Culture is often worth the premium if you eat cottage cheese plain or care about a thick, tangy, creamy texture. Daisy is the better value when you want a simple, versatile staple for blending, cooking, or topping, especially if price matters. Recent taste tests are split: Wirecutter and Allrecipes favored Good Culture’s flavor and curds, while Consumer Reports rated Daisy’s low-fat version highest overall. When to choose Good Culture Choose Good Culture if you’ll eat it straight from the container, with fruit, or on toast—uses where texture and flavor carry the experience. It tends to be thicker, creamier, and more tangy, with small tender curds; one review described its low-fat version as the best-tasting overall. Its organic versions use live active cultures and a short ingredient list, which may appeal if you prefer cultured, minimally formulated dairy. It is especially good as a spread or in recipes where a dense, ricotta-like consistency is useful, though some testers find it too thick for spooning. Expect to pay meaningfully more: one comparison put Good Culture at about 28 cents per ounce versus Daisy at about 17 cents per ounce. Best pick: Good Culture 2% Low-Fat Classic if you want a premium, tangy, creamy cottage cheese to eat on its own. When to choose Daisy Choose Daisy if you want dependable, inexpensive cottage cheese for everyday use—especially blended into smoothies, sauces, pancakes, lasagna, or protein bowls. Daisy has a very short ingredient list—cultured skim milk, cream, and salt in its classic version—and no gums or stabilizers. Consumer Reports gave its 2% low-fat small-curd version top marks, praising its simple ingredients, balanced salt, and milky sweetness. It has more distinct curds and a less integrated, less dense texture, which works well when you’re mixing it into something else. It is the better choice if you buy cottage cheese frequently and the price gap would add up. Best pick: Daisy 4% Cottage Cheese if you want a richer classic version for a lower price, or Daisy 2% if you want lower fat without sacrificing much. Quick decision guide Your priority Better choice Why Eating it plain Good Culture Tangier, creamier, more luxurious texture. Best value Daisy Lower price with a clean, simple ingredient list. Smoothies, baking, sauces Daisy Blends easily and is more cost-effective for recipes. Toast, bowls, or a ricotta-like spread Good Culture Thick texture works well as a spread. Distinct curds Daisy Its curds remain more separate rather than blended. Organic / live cultures Good Culture Its organic line emphasizes live active cultures and simple ingredients. For most people, I’d buy Daisy as the everyday default and keep Good Culture for when cottage cheese is the star—for example, a bowl with peaches and honey, or spread on toast. If you only buy one and price is not a concern, try Good Culture 2% first; if you go through multiple containers weekly, Daisy is probably the smarter recurring purchase.

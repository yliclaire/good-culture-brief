Retail prices are observed listings, not guaranteed local prices. Why these are better choices The key is lactose-free, not simply low-fat, organic, or probiotic. Ordinary cottage cheese can contain enough lactose to cause symptoms, even when it's made with cultured milk. Look for the following when shopping: “Lactose-free” on the package: This is the most important feature. Lactase enzyme in the ingredients: It breaks lactose down into easier-to-digest sugars. Protein content: Both Good Culture and Lactaid provide at least 13 g per serving. Sodium: Good Culture has slightly less sodium than Lactaid, although neither is particularly low-sodium. One important distinction: regular Good Culture cottage cheese is not lactose-free. Its regular 2% version contains approximately 4 g of lactose per serving, according to the manufacturer.

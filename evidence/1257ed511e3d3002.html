I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. Is Good Culture cottage cheese worth paying more for than Daisy? Explain when you would choose each.

Reviewed answer

It depends on what you value: for taste and cooking, Daisy holds its own, so the premium isn’t automatically worth it. Good Culture earns its higher price mainly if you care about sourcing, live cultures, or its particular texture. Both are simple products. Neither relies on the gums and stabilizers many cheaper brands use, which matters because some modern cottage cheese includes additives such as xanthan or guar gum to keep liquid from separating. Daisy is famously short on ingredients: cultured skim milk, cream, and salt. Good Culture is similar but adds live and active cultures after the curd is made, and the brand emphasizes pasture-raised milk, with organic versions available. Taste tests split. America’s Test Kitchen named Daisy 4% its winner in a blind tasting, ahead of Good Culture Organic, noting its tasters preferred drier, bolder, tangier cottage cheese with fewer ingredients. Other panels went the other way. One tasting picked Good Culture 4% as best overall for its fluffy, ricotta-like consistency, while naming Daisy 4% the creamiest, and a chef panel gave Good Culture Low-Fat two of three votes. So neither is objectively better; they’re just a bit different. Good Culture tends to have smaller, thicker, less soupy curds, and Daisy is richer and creamier with a classic tang. America's Test Kitchen LogoCook's Country LogoCook's Illustrated Logo +3 I’d choose Daisy when you’re cooking with it (lasagna, dips, egg bites, baking), buying in larger tubs, or just want good, clean cottage cheese at a lower price. It’s widely available and usually noticeably cheaper per ounce. I’d choose Good Culture when you eat it plain as a snack and prefer a thicker, less runny texture; when you want live probiotic cultures (most cottage cheese, including Daisy’s standard line, doesn’t keep them alive, since heating the curd kills the culture); when you want organic or pasture-raised milk; or when single-serve cups for grab-and-go protein are convenient. Practically, buy one of each and taste them side by side. If you can’t tell much difference, Daisy is the better value. Also check the protein and sodium per serving on the labels, since these vary by fat level and product line.

Reviewed answer

My top pick is Good Culture, with a couple of good alternatives depending on budget and priorities. Why Good Culture: Its whole line is built around live cultures. The company sources from pasture-raised cows and uses only milk, cream, and sea salt plus live and active cultures. It also does well in taste tests. EatingWell’s blind tasting named Good Culture’s Double Cream Classic best overall and its Low-Fat Classic best low-fat option, both with live and active cultures. The texture is a common reason people like it: it’s creamy without being runny and avoids the thickeners and gums some brands use. The main downside is price. It costs more than many other brands. Good alternatives: Breakstone’s Live-Active: The budget, widely available choice. It’s made from cultured milk with probiotics and has 13 grams of protein per ½ cup. Make sure you buy the “Live-Active” version, because Breakstone’s regular cottage cheese isn’t marketed for live cultures. Nancy’s Organic: Best if you want probiotics specifically. Its whole-milk version lists 4 strains of lactic cultures plus added live probiotic cultures, and the brand calls it fully cultured with a traditional tangy flavor. Westby or Kalona SuperNatural: Simple organic options. EatingWell named Westby’s Small Curd 4% Organic the best full-fat pick, and it has live active cultures. Kalona’s organic whole milk version is just milk, cream, Celtic Sea Salt, and live and active cultures. Check the label, whatever brand you choose. Many cottage cheeses don’t contain live cultures. Some are made with vinegar instead of bacterial cultures. Even cultured ones may not qualify, because the heating step kills the original cultures unless new strains are added afterward. Look for “live and active cultures” or “probiotic cultures” on the tub. Named strains are a bonus. So: Good Culture for the best overall product, Breakstone’s Live-Active if you want something cheaper and easy to find, and Nancy’s if probiotics are your top priority.

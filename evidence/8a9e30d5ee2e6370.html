My top pick is Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, with Lactaid Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese as a solid backup. Why Good Culture. It’s real dairy with the lactose broken down, made from five simple ingredients: pasture-raised milk, cream, sea salt, live and active cultures and lactase enzyme. The lactase enzyme is the key part: it breaks lactose into simple sugars your body can absorb, so you get normal cottage cheese without the digestive trouble. It also has 14 g of protein per serving, and no gums, carrageenan, or artificial additives. Many people find that gives it a thicker, more natural texture than typical brands. If you prefer organic, there’s also a lactose-free, whole milk organic version sold in 15 oz tubs and 5 oz single-serve cups, which first launched at Whole Foods. Good Culture Launches New Products Including Lactose Free Sour Cream And Cottage Cheese Line +2 Why Lactaid as the alternative. It’s the most familiar lactose-free dairy brand and is often a bit cheaper. Their cottage cheese is small curd, 1% milkfat, with 12g of protein, and labeled 100% lactose-free. Some reviewers say it tastes just like regular cottage cheese, but its overall ratings are mixed and stock varies from store to store. It’s worth checking your local store’s website before you go. A few practical tips: Check the label for “lactose-free.” Regular cottage cheese has more lactose than aged cheeses like cheddar or parmesan, so it’s one of the cheeses most likely to cause symptoms. Lactase pills are another option. If you’re mildly intolerant and your store doesn’t carry a lactose-free version, taking an over-the-counter lactase supplement with regular cottage cheese works for many people. This isn’t for a milk allergy. Lactose-free cottage cheese is still made from cow’s milk. If your problem is actually a milk allergy, you’d need a plant-based alternative instead.

When shopping for low-sodium cottage cheese in the United States, standard cottage cheese is notoriously high in salt (often containing 350 mg to 400+ mg of sodium per half-cup serving). To significantly cut back, look for specifically labeled "Low Sodium," "Reduced Sodium," or "No Salt Added" products. Top Recommendations Breakstone’s Reduced Sodium / Lowfat 2% Cottage Cheese Sodium Content: ~200 mg per half-cup serving. Why choose it: This is one of the most widely distributed national brands offering a dramatic drop in sodium. Standard cottage cheese typically hovers around 350–400 mg per serving, meaning this option cuts your sodium intake by roughly 45% while still maintaining the classic texture and protein content (~13g per serving). Regional Brands with "No Salt Added" Options (e.g., Hood, Friendship Dairies, or Lucerne) Sodium Content: Varies, but often significantly lower (some regional or specialty "no salt added" formulations drop close to natural dairy baseline levels, typically under 100–140 mg per serving). Why choose it: If you live in an area where regional brands like Hood (No Salt Added Low Fat) or Lucerne (found at Safeway/Albertsons affiliates) are available, they frequently offer true "no-salt-added" styles. These brands omit added salt entirely during manufacturing, relying only on the naturally occurring sodium inherent in milk. Grocery Store Private Labels Check major supermarket chains like Target (Good & Gather), Kroger, or Trader Joe's. Many of these stores carry their own low-fat or low-sodium lines that retail at a lower price point than national brands while matching the ~200 mg sodium benchmark. Shopping Tips Check the Nutrition Label First: Look specifically at the Milligrams (mg) of Sodium per Serving Size (usually 1/2 cup or 113g). Try to find options closer to the 140 mg mark or lower, which legally qualifies a food as "low sodium" by FDA standards. Beware of "Low Fat" Traps: "Low-fat" does not mean low sodium. Manufacturers often increase the sodium in low-fat or fat-free cottage cheese to compensate for flavor loss. Always verify the sodium line item independently of the fat content.

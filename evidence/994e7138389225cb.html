Choose Hood No Salt Added Low Fat Cottage Cheese if you can find it. It has 60 mg sodium per 1/2-cup serving, no added salt, and 14 g protein—far below typical cottage cheese. Best picks Product Sodium per 1/2 cup Why choose it Hood No Salt Added Low Fat Cottage Cheese 60 mg Explicitly no added salt; also low fat and high protein. Lucerne Low-Fat, 1% Milkfat Cottage Cheese 60 mg A widely cited no-added-salt option; 12 g protein per serving. A store-brand “no salt added” / reduced-sodium cottage cheese Roughly 45–200 mg Often the most accessible option; check the Nutrition Facts panel rather than relying on front-of-package wording. Why this matters Regular cottage cheese commonly contains about 300–450 mg sodium per half cup, so a large serving can consume a substantial share of a 2,300 mg daily limit. If you have hypertension, heart or kidney disease, a clinician may advise a lower daily sodium target—often around 1,500–2,000 mg—making the low-sodium versions especially worthwhile. What to look for Pick a label that says “no salt added” or “reduced sodium,” then confirm the number on the Nutrition Facts panel. Aim for roughly under 100 mg per 1/2 cup if sodium is a major concern. Favor low-fat or 1% versions if you also want less saturated fat; they still provide substantial protein. If only regular cottage cheese is available, rinsing the curds in a strainer can remove some surface salt, though it will not match a true no-salt-added product. Bottom line: Buy Hood No Salt Added Low Fat Cottage Cheese first; Lucerne’s low-fat no-added-salt version is an excellent alternative. Both deliver the protein benefit of cottage cheese with only about 60 mg sodium per half cup.

When shopping for cottage cheese in the U.S., Good Culture and Daisy are widely considered the two gold standards, largely because both avoid the gums, thickeners, and artificial preservatives found in many store brands. Whether Good Culture is "worth" paying more for—it typically costs roughly 30% to 50% more than Daisy—depends entirely on your culinary priorities, texture preferences, and budget. Nutritional & Ingredient Head-to-Head Feature Good Culture Daisy Typical Price (16 oz) ~$4.39 – $4.99+ ~$3.00 – $3.99 Ingredients Skim milk, whole milk, cream, sea salt, live and active cultures, microbial enzymes Skim milk, cream, salt, vitamin A palmitate, microbial enzymes Protein (per 1/2 cup) 14g 13g Probiotics Contains live and active probiotic cultures Standard dairy cultures (not marketed as probiotic) Choose Good Culture If: You prioritize protein and macros: Good Culture edges out slightly higher protein content with marginally fewer carbohydrates per serving. You want gut-health benefits: It includes live and active probiotic cultures added to the mix. You love a thick, savory, and tart flavor profile: Good Culture is famous for its distinct, slightly tangy "twang" and smaller, meatier curds suspended in a rich, velvety cream. You eat it plain or use it in savory dishes: Because of its rich tang and thick texture, it tastes incredible on its own, sprinkled with black pepper, or mixed into savory recipes (like grain bowls or scrambled eggs). Choose Daisy If: You want the best value for your money: Daisy delivers exceptional, clean-ingredient quality at a notably lower price point. You prefer a milder, classic taste: Daisy has a very clean, fresh, milky flavor without the pronounced tang or saltiness that some critics find polarizing in Good Culture. You prefer softer, traditional curds: Daisy's curds tend to feel a bit more uniform and classic, paired with a soupier, milkier liquid base. You use cottage cheese primarily as a blank canvas: If you are blending cottage cheese into smoothies, mixing it with sweet fruit and honey, or folding it into baking recipes, Daisy's neutral flavor profile lets the other ingredients shine without adding an extra fermented tang. The Verdict If budget is tight or you use cottage cheese mostly as an ingredient or sweet snack, Daisy is hard to beat. However, if you eat cottage cheese straight out of the tub as a daily high-protein ritual and love a thick, tangy, probiotic-rich bite, Good Culture is usually worth the splurge.

To minimize your cost per serving while eating cottage cheese regularly in the U.S., your best bet is to skip the premium name brands (like Good Culture or Organic Valley) and buy major grocery store private-label (store) brands. Specific recommendations and strategies to maximize your value include: 1. The Winner: Major Grocery Store Brands (e.g., Walmart's Great Value or Aldi's Friendly Farms) The Cost: Usually ranges between 10 to 14 cents per ounce, whereas name brands like Daisy or Knudsen frequently sit around 20 to 25 cents per ounce. Why buy it: Store brands are consistently the cheapest per serving. For example, Walmart’s Great Value Lowfat Cottage Cheese or Aldi’s Friendly Farms regularly rank well in blind taste tests for texture and flavor while undercutting competitors by nearly half the price. Other strong contenders: Trader Joe’s store brand and regional supermarket labels (like Kroger, Meijer, or Publix) also offer massive savings compared to specialty names. 2. Size Matters: Buy the Largest Tub Available If you eat cottage cheese regularly, never buy the 16 oz tubs or single-serve cups, which carry a steep markup. Look for 24 oz or 32 oz (or larger 48 oz) tubs. The cost-per-ounce drops significantly the larger the container goes, scaling down your expense per serving dramatically. 3. Watch Out for the "Protein Trap" Specialty "high-protein" or "lactose-free" brands (like Lactaid or specialized athletic lines) command a heavy price premium (often 25¢+ per ounce). Traditional, standard cottage cheese is already a powerhouse of protein (typically 12g to 14g per half-cup serving). Buying generic standard low-fat (2%) cottage cheese gives you nearly identical nutritional macros for a fraction of the cost. Summary Recommendation: Grab the largest store-brand tub (such as Walmart Great Value or Aldi Friendly Farms) available at your local supermarket. It yields the absolute lowest cost per serving without sacrificing the core protein and nutritional benefits you look for.

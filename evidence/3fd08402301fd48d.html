To minimize your cost per serving while eating cottage cheese regularly in the U.S., your best bet is to bypass premium specialty labels and buy major supermarket house brands (private labels), specifically Walmart's Great Value or regional equivalents. The Cost Breakdown & Strategy Brand Category Typical Price (per 16 oz equivalent) Cost per Oz Why Buy (or Avoid) Store Brands (e.g., Walmart Great Value, Trader Joe's, regional grocery chains) ~$2.50 to $2.99 (often sold in larger 24 oz tubs for even lower unit costs like ~$2.87) ~11¢ to 15¢ per oz The absolute cheapest. House brands are manufactured by major commercial dairies, offering identical nutritional profiles (protein-to-calorie ratio) to name brands at a fraction of the cost. Affordable National Brands (e.g., Daisy) ~$3.00 to $3.99 ~19¢ to 25¢ per oz Great clean ingredient list (no gums or thickeners), but you pay a marketing markup. Premium / Specialty Brands (e.g., Good Culture, Organic Valley) ~$4.50 to $7.00+ ~28¢ to 44¢+ per oz High quality with added probiotics or organic sourcing, but terrible for a budget if you eat it regularly. Key Recommendations for Maximizing Savings Go for the 24 oz (or larger) tubs: If you eat cottage cheese regularly, avoid standard 16 oz containers. Larger institutional or family-size tubs (like Walmart's 24 oz Great Value container or club store bulk sizes from Costco/Sam's Club) drastically lower the per-ounce price. Stick to Private Labels: Trader Joe's, Aldi (Friendly Farms), Target (Good & Gather), and Walmart (Great Value) sell cottage cheese produced by major regional dairies. Nutritionally, standard cottage cheese is heavily regulated and tightly standardized—meaning store-brand low-fat or whole-milk options deliver the exact same protein punch (around 13–14g of protein per 1/2 cup serving) as expensive labels. Watch out for "Gums & Thickeners" in cheap brands: While store brands win on price, check the ingredient label if you care about texture. Some ultra-cheap brands use guar gum or xanthan gum to mimic creaminess. If you prefer a cleaner ingredient list without paying premium prices, Daisy is usually the most reasonably priced "simple ingredient" national brand, but store brands will still win on raw cost-per-serving.

Reviewed answer

My pick for a high-protein snack is Good Culture, with Daisy as a close runner-up. Why Good Culture: It leads on protein in the snack-size format. The Classic 2% single-serve cup is 5.3 oz with 19 g of protein and is made from pasture-raised milk. The ingredient list is short (milk, cream, salt, live cultures), and it’s thicker and less watery than many brands, which makes it more satisfying to eat straight from the cup. The single-serve size works well for grab-and-go snacking. The trade-off is price: it usually costs more per ounce than mainstream brands. Why Daisy is a strong alternative: It’s cheaper, sold almost everywhere, and has an equally clean ingredient list (cultured skim milk, cream, salt). Its 5.3 oz cups have 17 g of protein in both the 2% and 4% versions. If you’d rather buy a large tub and portion it yourself, Daisy is probably the best value for the protein. A few tips for comparing labels in the store: Check the serving size. Cups are often 5.3 oz, but tubs list protein per ½ cup, which is usually around 12 to 14 g. That makes cups look higher in protein even when the cottage cheese is similar. Choose plain over fruit-on-the-bottom. The fruit versions have less protein per ounce and more sugar. Look at the sodium. Cottage cheese can be fairly salty, so compare brands if that matters to you. Pick the fat level you like. 2% and 4% have almost the same protein, so choose based on taste. 4% tastes richer, and 2% or nonfat saves a few calories. For an easy snack upgrade, add berries and a drizzle of honey for something sweet, or everything-bagel seasoning and cherry tomatoes for something savory.

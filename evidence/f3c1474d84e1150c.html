Choose LACTAID® Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese as the safest, most widely available U.S. option. It is real dairy cottage cheese with added lactase enzyme, which breaks lactose into glucose and galactose that are easier to digest; it also provides 13 g of protein per ½ cup. Best choice Product Why choose it Watch for LACTAID® Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, 4% milkfat Explicitly lactose-free, widely sold in U.S. grocery stores, and uses lactase to digest the milk sugar. It is a 4% milkfat product, so it is richer than low-fat versions. Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, 2% milkfat A good alternative if you prefer a thick, high-protein style; Good Culture sells a specifically lactose-free 2% version. Availability can be more store-dependent than LACTAID. Why not ordinary cottage cheese? Regular cottage cheese is not lactose-free. It generally contains about 1–6 g of lactose per serving—less than milk, but enough to trigger symptoms for some people with lactose intolerance. If you are only mildly intolerant, a small serving of regular cottage cheese may be tolerable, but a product labeled “lactose-free” removes the guesswork. What to look for Pick a package that says “lactose-free” on the front, not merely “low-fat” or “high-protein.” LACTAID is the clearest mainstream pick because its lactase enzyme is specifically intended to make the dairy easier to digest. If you want a creamier texture, choose the LACTAID 4% version; if you want less fat, look for Good Culture’s lactose-free 2% version. Check sodium if that matters to you; dietitians often suggest choosing cottage cheeses with roughly 300 mg or less sodium per serving. Bottom line: Buy LACTAID® Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese if you want the safest and easiest-to-find option; choose Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese if you prefer its thicker, higher-protein style.

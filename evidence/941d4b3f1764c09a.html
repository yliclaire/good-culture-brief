Choose LACTAID® Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese as the safest, most widely available U.S. option. It is regular dairy cottage cheese with added lactase enzyme, which breaks lactose into easier-to-digest sugars, so it is specifically designed for lactose intolerance. Best pick Product Why choose it Watch for LACTAID® Cottage Cheese, 4% milkfat, 16 oz Explicitly lactose-free; contains lactase enzyme; 13 g protein per 1/2-cup serving; sold widely at U.S. retailers including Target. It is a higher-fat, creamier style; check sodium if that matters to you. Good Culture Simply Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, 2% milkfat, 15 oz Also explicitly lactose-free, with a thick, high-protein cottage-cheese style. Availability can be more store-dependent than LACTAID. Why not ordinary cottage cheese? Regular cottage cheese still contains lactose—typically more than aged hard cheeses—so it may cause symptoms depending on your personal tolerance. Many people with lactose intolerance can handle modest amounts of lactose, but a labeled lactose-free product removes the guesswork. What to look for “Lactose-free” on the front label—this is the key phrase. Lactase enzyme in the ingredient list—LACTAID uses this to digest the milk’s lactose before you do. Avoid assuming “cultured” or “probiotic” means lactose-free; those terms do not guarantee low lactose. If you want a non-dairy option instead, look for a plant-based cottage-cheese alternative, but note that lactose intolerance is different from a milk allergy: lactose-free dairy is generally appropriate for lactose intolerance, while dairy allergy requires avoiding dairy entirely. For a simple shopping decision in [name and location withheld]: buy LACTAID 4% Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese. If you prefer a lighter, thicker texture and find it in stock, Good Culture Simply Lactose-Free 2% is an excellent alternative.

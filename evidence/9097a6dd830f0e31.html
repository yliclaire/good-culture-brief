I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. Is Good Culture cottage cheese worth paying more for than Daisy? Explain when you would choose each.

Good Culture is worth the extra cost if you care about probiotics, sourcing, or a thicker, tangier texture. If you mostly want clean, inexpensive protein, Daisy is hard to beat. What they have in common Both are among the simplest cottage cheeses on U.S. shelves. Neither uses the gums, stabilizers, or thickeners (like guar or xanthan gum) found in many store brands. Daisy is famously just cultured skim milk, cream, and salt. Good Culture’s ingredient list is similarly short. You aren’t paying Good Culture’s premium to escape additives; Daisy already does that. Where Good Culture differs Live and active cultures. Good Culture adds probiotic cultures and markets this heavily. Daisy is cultured during production but isn’t sold as a probiotic food. This is the strongest reason to pay more if gut health matters to you. Sourcing. Good Culture uses milk from pasture-raised cows and sells organic versions. Daisy is conventional. Texture and taste. Good Culture is thicker, drier, and tangier, with firmer curds. Daisy is milder, creamier, and a bit looser. Protein. Both are high-protein. Good Culture tends to run slightly higher per serving, but the gap is small. Check labels for the specific fat level you’re comparing. Price Good Culture usually costs noticeably more per ounce, often around 30–60% more. Daisy also comes in larger tubs that bring the per-ounce price down further. Exact prices vary a lot by store and region, so compare the unit price on the shelf tag. When I’d choose Daisy You eat cottage cheese daily and cost adds up. You’re cooking or baking with it, like lasagna, protein pancakes, or blended sauces, where texture and probiotics don’t matter much. You prefer a mild, creamy cottage cheese. You want a clean ingredient list without paying extra for it. When I’d choose Good Culture You want probiotics from your food. Organic or pasture-raised dairy is a priority. You eat it plain or as a snack and like a thick, tangy, Greek-yogurt-like texture. You want the single-serve cups for convenience. The honest bottom line: nutritionally, the two are close, and Daisy is one of the best values in the dairy aisle. Good Culture’s premium buys probiotics, sourcing standards, and a different texture. If none of those matter to you, Daisy is the smarter buy.

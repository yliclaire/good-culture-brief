Buy Good Culture 2% Simply Cottage Cheese if you want the best all-around choice: it has a short ingredient list (milk, cream, and sea salt), live active cultures, a creamy but not watery texture, and strong protein content. Multiple chef and expert roundups independently named it their top grocery-store pick. Why it stands out Cleaner formula: It avoids the gums and stabilizers found in many mainstream cottage cheeses, which often improve shelf stability but can make texture gummy or overly processed. Good texture and flavor: Reviewers and chefs highlight its fresh, tangy, creamy profile and substantial curds. Practical nutrition: Cottage cheese is generally high in protein; a half-cup of 2% cottage cheese provides about 12 g of protein, and Good Culture’s live cultures may be a plus if you want fermented dairy. Best alternatives If you want… Buy Why Widely available, simple, classic Daisy 4% Cottage Cheese Only milk, cream, and salt; no stabilizers or thickeners; about 13 g protein per half-cup. Budget option Trader Joe’s Low-Fat Small Curd Dietitian-recommended for solid nutrition, taste, and texture at a lower price point. Maximum protein Mulu Formulated with added whey protein and available at Walmart. My practical recommendation For [name and location withheld], start with Good Culture 2% if you see it at Target, Wegmans, Harris Teeter, or another nearby grocer. If you prefer a more traditional, mildly sweet cottage cheese or want the simplest possible label, choose Daisy 4% instead. Avoid choosing based only on “low-fat”: the calorie gap between nonfat and 4% is only about 30 calories per serving, while full-fat versions tend to taste richer and more satisfying.

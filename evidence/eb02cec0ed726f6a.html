Buy Good Culture Simply Cottage Cheese (2% milkfat) if you want the best all-around U.S. grocery-store cottage cheese. It is repeatedly favored by chefs and dietitians for its creamy, non-watery texture, fresh tang, high protein, and short ingredient list—milk, cream, and sea salt—without gums or stabilizers. Why Good Culture Texture and flavor: Chefs describe it as creamy without being watery, with a balanced tang and substantial curds. Clean label: Its simple formulation avoids the added thickeners and stabilizers common in some competitors. Nutrition: It uses live active cultures and offers strong protein, making it a good choice for breakfast bowls, snacks, or savory dishes. Availability: It is widely carried at mainstream U.S. retailers, including Target. Best alternatives If you want… Buy Why The cheapest clean-label option Daisy 4% Cottage Cheese Only milk, cream, and salt; no stabilizers or thickeners, with about 13 g protein per half-cup. A lower-fat everyday option Daisy Low-Fat 2% Small Curd It was one of Consumer Reports’ top picks, alongside Good Culture. The best traditional, dense style Friendship Dairies 4% Small Curd California Style Wirecutter’s top pick: fine, ricotta-like curds with a tangy, sweet dairy finish. A budget option Trader Joe’s Low-Fat Small Curd A dietitian-recommended value pick with good nutrition and texture. Quick buying rule Choose 2% for a good balance of creaminess and lower saturated fat; choose 4% if you want a richer, more traditional cottage cheese. The calorie difference between nonfat and 4% is modest—roughly 30 calories per serving—so texture and ingredient quality matter more than chasing the lowest fat. For [name and location withheld], I’d start with Good Culture 2%; if it is unavailable or you want to spend less, get Daisy 2% or 4%.

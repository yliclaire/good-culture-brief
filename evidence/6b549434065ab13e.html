For a first purchase, I’d get Good Culture Low-Fat Classic Cottage Cheese (2% milkfat). It’s a widely available U.S. brand with a clean, mild flavor and a creamy-but-not-heavy texture, making it an easy introduction before you decide whether you prefer richer 4% styles. Why this one Approachable texture: Cottage cheese can surprise first-timers because of its curds; Good Culture’s classic style is generally praised for being smooth and balanced rather than watery or overly tangy. Good starting fat level: A nutritionist cited by Consumer Reports specifically recommends starting with 2% cottage cheese, then moving to 4% if you want something richer and creamier. Versatile: It works equally well sweet—with fruit, honey, or granola—or savory—with pepper, tomatoes, avocado, or on toast. Reliable reviews: Independent taste tests have repeatedly placed Good Culture among the top cottage-cheese options. What to look for on the label Label Best for 2% milkfat First try; lighter, balanced flavor 4% milkfat Creamier, richer experience Small-curd Smoother, more uniform texture Large-curd More distinct, chewier curds If Good Culture isn’t nearby Choose Daisy Pure & Natural Low-Fat Cottage Cheese as a close alternative; it was named best overall in a large taste test and is commonly stocked in U.S. grocery stores. If you already know you enjoy creamy dairy foods, consider a 4% option such as Hood Country Style, which won a creaminess-focused taste test. doordash For your first tub, buy a small container, try it plain first, then add a little salt, black pepper, and sliced peach or pineapple—cottage cheese is much better when lightly seasoned.

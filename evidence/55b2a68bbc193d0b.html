For a first tub, I’d recommend Daisy Low-Fat 2% Milkfat Small Curd Cottage Cheese. It’s widely available in U.S. grocery stores, has a very short ingredient list, and is a balanced, approachable introduction to the category—mildly tangy, lightly sweet, and not overly rich. Why Daisy is the best first choice Gentle, familiar flavor: Consumer Reports’ testers described it as having a sour-cream-like flavor, balanced saltiness, and natural milky sweetness—more approachable than very tangy or very dense options. Simple ingredients: Its ingredients are essentially cultured skim milk, cream, and salt, which is a good starting point if you want to learn what plain cottage cheese tastes like. Good texture for beginners: It has uniform, slightly firm curds and a creamy consistency, without being as thick or ricotta-like as some “high-protein” cottage cheeses. A sensible fat level: A Consumer Reports nutritionist specifically recommends starting with 2% cottage cheese, then moving to 4% if you decide you want something richer and creamier. How to try it first Start with a small spoonful cold, straight from the fridge. If the texture feels unfamiliar, pair it with something you already like: Sweet: pineapple, peaches, berries, or a drizzle of honey Savory: cherry tomatoes, cucumber, black pepper, everything-bagel seasoning, or on toast High-protein upgrade: blend it into scrambled eggs or use it as a creamy base for fruit If Daisy isn’t available Choose Breakstone’s 2% Milkfat Lowfat Small Curd as a close alternative; Consumer Reports rated it highly for a creamy, buttery flavor with mild tang, though it contains tapioca starch for a thicker texture. If you prefer a thicker, spoonable style for toast or bagels, Good Culture Organic 2% Low-Fat Classic is another strong option. One practical note: cottage cheese can be salty—some versions exceed 400 mg sodium per half cup—so check the label if you’re watching sodium intake.

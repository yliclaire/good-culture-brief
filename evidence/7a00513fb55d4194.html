Reviewed answer

Reviewed answer text; personal name/location references are withheld where present. Original interface captures and screenshots are retained in the private research repository. Product claims are not independently fact-verified.

My top pick is Good Culture Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese, mainly because it’s the easiest to find and has a short, clean ingredient list. Good Culture makes it the way lactose-free milk is made: the cottage cheese has 14 grams of protein per serving and uses only pasture-raised milk, cream, sea salt, live cultures, and lactase enzyme. The lactase breaks down the lactose for you, so you get real dairy taste without the symptoms. It’s also 2% milkfat and free of gums, carrageenan, and artificial ingredients, which matters to some people because gums and thickeners can cause stomach trouble of their own. If you’d like organic, Good Culture also makes an organic, whole-milk lactose-free version in 15 oz tubs and 5 oz single-serve cups. The organic one launched at Whole Foods, so check there or at larger supermarkets like Kroger, Target, or Harris Teeter. Good Culture Launches New Sour Cream and Cottage Cheese Products +2 Lactaid Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese is the other well-known option. It’s a 1% milkfat, small-curd cottage cheese with 12g of protein, and people like it because it tastes like classic cottage cheese. It can be hard to find, though. Walmart’s listing currently shows it as unavailable online, so look in stores before you count on it. A few tips while you shop: Read the label. Only buy tubs that say “lactose-free.” Regular cottage cheese still has a fair amount of lactose (often a few grams per serving), unlike aged hard cheeses such as cheddar or parmesan, which have almost none. Lactase pills are a backup. If you only find regular cottage cheese, an over-the-counter lactase supplement taken with your first bite works well for many people. Watch for milk allergies. Lactose-free products are still made from cow’s milk. That’s fine for lactose intolerance, but not for anyone with a milk protein allergy.

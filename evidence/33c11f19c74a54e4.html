For a high-protein snack in the U.S., the top recommendation depends on whether you are looking for absolute maximum protein efficiency or the best overall balance of taste, texture, and clean ingredients. 1. The Ultimate Macro Winner: MULU High-Protein Cottage Cheese Protein per ½ cup (113g): 18g Calories: 100 Why buy it: Launched nationally by Dairy Farmers of America, MULU is built specifically for the modern protein seeker. Using an ultra-filtering process, it packs a massive 18 grams of complete dairy protein into just 100 calories with only 4 grams of carbohydrates. If your primary goal is maximizing protein density per calorie (rivaling Greek yogurt while maintaining a thick texture), this is currently the market leader. 2. The Cult Favorite & Best Overall: Good Culture (2% Low-Fat Classic) Protein per ½ cup (113g): 14g Calories: 110–120 (depending on exact style) Why buy it: While it has slightly fewer grams of protein than specialized ultra-filtered lines, Good Culture is widely considered the gold standard of U.S. cottage cheeses. Clean Ingredients: It uses pasture-raised milk, live and active cultures (probiotics), and avoids gums, thickeners, or artificial additives. Texture & Taste: It features a remarkably thick, velvety, and creamy texture with a mild, pleasant tang rather than an overly watery or metallic bite. 3. Best Runner-Up / Widely Available: Daisy (Low-Fat 2%) Protein per ½ cup (113g): 13g Calories: 110 Why buy it: If you can't find Good Culture or MULU locally, Daisy is stocked in nearly every grocery store across the country. It contains a minimal ingredient list (milk, cream, salt, and cultures) and boasts a classic, clean taste with a thick, satisfying whey-to-curd ratio. Summary Recommendation Choose MULU if you want the absolute highest protein-to-calorie ratio for fitness goals or strict calorie deficits. Choose Good Culture if you prioritize superior, clean-label taste, texture, and gut-friendly live cultures as an everyday snack. Are you tracking specific macros (like low sodium or low fat) for your snack, or looking for a specific type of curd size?

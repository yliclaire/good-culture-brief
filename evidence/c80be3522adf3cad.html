My top recommendation is Good Culture Simply Lactose Free Cottage Cheese (2% milkfat). It's a particularly good choice if you're lactose intolerant but still want the protein, creamy texture, and nutritional benefits of traditional cottage cheese. The most important thing is to buy a product explicitly labeled "lactose-free," rather than simply "low-fat," "cultured," or "probiotic." Those descriptions don't guarantee that the lactose has been removed or broken down. Best cottage cheese options in the U.S. My top pick Good Culture Simply Lactose Free Cottage Cheese, 2% 14 g protein per ½ cup, 90 calories, and 0 g added sugar. Contains lactase enzyme, live cultures, and no gums or thickeners. Best for: High protein, simple ingredients, and lower fat. 15 oz tub · Lactaid Cottage Cheese, 16 oz A lactose-free, full-fat alternative with about 13 g protein per serving. Good if you prefer richer cottage cheese or want an accessible supermarket option. Best for: Everyday shopping and a creamier, full-fat option. 16 oz · $3.38 at Walmart (listed price, varies by store) Green Valley Creamery Organic Lactose-Free Cottage Cheese 12 g protein per ½ cup, 110 calories, organic dairy ingredients, and lactase enzyme. Its formula avoids gums and stabilizers. Best for: Shoppers who prioritize organic ingredients and a certified FODMAP-friendly product. 12 oz · Nutrition from manufacturer product labels. Prices and local availability can change. Why these products are better for lactose intolerance The key ingredient is lactase enzyme. Manufacturers add it to break down lactose into simpler sugars that are easier to digest. You still get real dairy protein without the usual lactose load. One important distinction: lactose-free doesn't mean sugar-free. Good Culture's lactose-free cottage cheese lists 4 g total sugars, but those sugars are not necessarily lactose. Here's how I would choose: Best overall: Good Culture Lactose Free 2% for high protein, simpler ingredients, and lower saturated fat. Best conventional supermarket choice: Lactaid Cottage Cheese. Its ingredient list is longer, including gums and preservatives, but it is specifically formulated for lactose intolerance. Best organic option: Green Valley Creamery, if you can find its cottage cheese. Its current product selection makes availability less certain. What I would avoid I wouldn't make regular Daisy, standard Good Culture, or other cottage cheeses without a lactose-free label your first choice. Even products containing live cultures can still have substantial lactose. If you're very sensitive, start with a small portion of a lactose-free version and see how you tolerate it. The NIH notes that lactose tolerance varies considerably from person to person. Also, lactose-free cottage cheese still contains milk proteins and is not safe for someone with a milk allergy. Bottom line: Buy Good Culture Simply Lactose Free 2% if you can find it. Otherwise, Lactaid Cottage Cheese is a solid alternative. I wouldn't pay a large premium for one over the other solely for lactose intolerance; both are designed to address that problem.

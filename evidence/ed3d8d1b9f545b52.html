Good Culture is worth paying more for if you love its thicker, denser texture or specifically want organic dairy. But Daisy is the better everyday value for most people. Both have short ingredient lists, plenty of protein, and live cultures. Good Culture isn't dramatically healthier. One important shopping detail: Good Culture sells both a regular Simply version and a more expensive Organic version. The distinction matters because regular Good Culture can cost about the same as Daisy. Good Culture vs. Daisy at a glance Good Culture Best for thick texture, eating plain, and organic options Daisy Best for classic creaminess, everyday use, and value Comparing their regular 4% milkfat varieties: Per approximately ½ cup Good Culture Simply Daisy Calories 100 110 Protein 14 g 13 g Total fat 4.5 g 5 g Sugar 2 g 4 g Sodium 380 mg 370 mg Live cultures Yes Yes Added gums or starches No No Manufacturer and retailer nutrition labels; servings are 110 g and 113 g, respectively. The nutritional differences are modest. The extra gram of protein in Good Culture isn't enough by itself to justify a substantial premium, and Daisy's slightly higher sugar content comes from naturally occurring dairy sugars, not added sugar. What does the extra money actually buy? Good Culture: thick and concentrated Dense, small curds with a tangier, cheese-forward impression. Less loose dressing makes it especially appealing straight from the tub, spread on toast, or in a savory breakfast. Daisy: soft and creamy A more traditional, moist cottage cheese with a milder dairy flavor. Excellent with fruit, in bowls, or mixed into recipes where the dense texture of Good Culture would be less noticeable. Taste is subjective, but Daisy has independent support: in an October 2026 report on Consumer Reports' tasting, Daisy 2% was among the top picks, while Good Culture was noted for its dense, ricotta-like consistency. What about price? Current online U.S. listings illustrate why I wouldn't automatically assume Good Culture is a premium-priced purchase. Target's listings even show both regular 16 oz varieties at $3.99, while Good Culture Organic is $6.59. Prices depend on store and location. When I'd choose each Choose Good Culture when eating it plain is the main event, you prefer a very thick texture, or you want its specifically labeled organic version. Its extra protein is a minor bonus. Choose Daisy when making smoothies, pancakes, egg bakes, or dips; when buying larger quantities; or when Good Culture costs significantly more. Daisy also has a clean, minimal ingredient list. Either is excellent for a high-protein snack. Both contain live cultures, but neither should be assumed to deliver clinically proven probiotic benefits merely because of that labeling. My price rule How much more does Good Culture cost per 16 oz tub? $1.00 more 25¢ extra per ½ cup $0 $3 I'd pay this premium for eating it plain, not for cooking. The nutritional difference alone doesn't justify the extra cost. Bottom line: I would buy Good Culture Simply when its price is close to Daisy's and I plan to enjoy the cottage cheese itself. I would buy Daisy when it's notably cheaper, especially for everyday meals and cooking. I wouldn't pay an organic-level premium for Good Culture expecting a major nutritional improvement.

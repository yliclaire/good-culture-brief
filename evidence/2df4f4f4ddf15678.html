Reviewed answer

Choose Good Culture Simply 2% Low-Fat Classic Cottage Cheese (or its 4% version if you prefer richer dairy). It explicitly contains live and active cultures, including Lactobacillus paracasei, and it also has a simple ingredient list, high protein, and strong taste/texture reviews. Why Good Culture Live and active cultures: The 2% Classic contains live and active Lactobacillus paracasei cultures, which is exactly what you’re looking for. Simple ingredients: Its listed ingredients are skim milk, whole milk, cream, sea salt, and live and active cultures—no added sugar or artificial ingredients. Nutrition: A half-cup serving provides 14 g protein, 100 calories, and 340 mg sodium. Quality reputation: Independent taste tests have named Good Culture a top cottage-cheese pick for its creamy texture, balanced tang, and protein content. Alternatives Brand Cultures? Best for Note Good Culture Simply 2% or 4% Yes—live and active cultures Best overall choice Tangier, thick, creamy, high protein. Daisy Low-Fat 2% Cultured milk, but check the label for a “live and active cultures” claim Minimal ingredients Ingredients are cultured skim milk, cream, and salt; it is not the same explicit live-culture claim as Good Culture. Nancy’s Organic Probiotic Cottage Cheese Yes—probiotic Yogurt-like tang A good organic/probiotic-focused alternative. What to check on the label Look for the phrase “live and active cultures” or a named culture such as Lactobacillus paracasei. “Cultured milk” alone means the product was fermented, but it does not necessarily guarantee that live cultures remain in the finished tub. If your priority is specifically live cultures, buy Good Culture; if you want organic plus probiotics, consider Nancy’s.

I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I want cottage cheese with live and active cultures. Which brand should I choose, and why?

My pick would be Good Culture, with Nancy’s as a close alternative if you care most about probiotics. First, why the brand matters: most cottage cheese is heated during production, and that cooking step can eliminate the very cultures that started the fermentation process. When you see “live and active cultures” on a label, it usually means cultures were added back after the heating step, often in the creamy dressing. Some brands are made by only using vinegar, so “cultured milk” alone doesn’t guarantee live bacteria. Good Culture is the best all-around choice. Its ingredient list is short: organic skim milk, organic whole milk, organic cream, sea salt, and live and active cultures. It also tastes good. In one 13-brand taste test, Good Culture Organic Whole Milk Classic Cottage Cheese came out on top, and testers liked the gentle tang the cultures give it. It’s sold in most major grocery chains. The main drawback is that it is more expensive than many of the other brands. Nancy’s Organic is the best choice if probiotics are your main goal. It contains 4 strains of lactic cultures, and live probiotic cultures, and is made without rennet or thickeners. It is noticeably tangier, and it’s easiest to find on the West Coast and at natural-food stores. There are other good options too. Breakstone’s Live-Active is a cheaper option that’s widely available, and one taste test ranked its live active 2% cottage cheese at the top. Buy the “Live-Active” line specifically, not regular Breakstone’s. Kalona SuperNatural and Westby Organic also have simple ingredient lists with live and active cultures. Whichever tub you choose, check that the label actually says “live and active cultures” or “probiotic cultures.” Products within one brand can differ. It’s also worth knowing that “contains live cultures” and “clinically studied probiotic” aren’t the same claim. Cultured cottage cheese is a good addition to your diet, but it hasn’t been researched as thoroughly as yogurt.

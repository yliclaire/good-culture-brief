Good Culture is worth paying a little more for if you eat cottage cheese on its own and prefer a thick, tangy, high-protein snack. But Daisy is an excellent value—and its ingredients are just as clean. My choice would be Good Culture for eating straight from the container and Daisy for everyday cooking, mixing into recipes, or buying in larger quantities. When I would choose each Choose Good Culture when… You eat cottage cheese plain, with fruit, or on toast. You enjoy a denser, tangier, yogurt-like experience with small, soft curds. You want slightly more protein per serving. You're paying around $0.50–$1 extra per tub or less. You specifically want certified organic dairy, in which case select Good Culture Organic. Choose Daisy when… You prefer a mild, creamy, traditional cottage cheese. You're blending it into sauces, adding it to eggs, or baking with it. You eat cottage cheese frequently and can save by buying larger tubs. You want a short ingredient list without paying for premium branding. Good Culture costs substantially more at your grocery store. One misconception worth clearing up: Daisy is not a highly processed, additive-heavy alternative. Its regular cottage cheese uses cultured skim milk, cream, and salt, with live and active cultures. Good Culture also uses straightforward dairy ingredients and cultures. Neither has a demonstrated major gut-health advantage just because of its branding. How much extra is worth it? My verdict For the average U.S. shopper, I would buy Good Culture Simply if the price difference is small, especially for breakfast or a high-protein snack. Its thicker texture is the main reason to upgrade—not its modest nutritional advantage. But if Good Culture costs $5.99 and Daisy costs $3.99, I'd usually choose Daisy. You still get substantial protein, minimal ingredients, and live cultures. The smartest move is to try their 4% versions side by side. If Good Culture's texture doesn't noticeably improve your enjoyment, there's little nutritional reason to pay more.

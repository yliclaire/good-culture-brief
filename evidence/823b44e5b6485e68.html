Reviewed answer

Choose Hood No Salt Added Low Fat Cottage Cheese if you can find it: it has 60 mg sodium per ½-cup serving, with 14 g protein and no added salt or sugar—far below typical cottage cheese. A comparable option is Lucerne Low-Fat, 1% Milkfat Cottage Cheese, which is made without added salt and also has about 60 mg sodium per ½ cup. Why it’s the better pick Regular cottage cheese is often surprisingly salty: a ½-cup serving commonly contains roughly 300–450 mg sodium, or 15–20% of the 2,300 mg daily limit. By contrast, Hood’s no-salt-added version provides about 3% of the daily value per serving. What to look for Pick a label that says “no salt added,” “reduced sodium,” or “low sodium”—not just “low fat.” Check the sodium per ½-cup serving; aim for around 60–200 mg if sodium is a priority. Keep the serving size in mind: two cups of regular cottage cheese can contribute a substantial amount of sodium. If only regular cottage cheese is available, rinsing the curds in a strainer can remove some surface salt. If you have hypertension, heart disease, or kidney disease, a clinician or registered dietitian can help set an individual sodium target; general guidance is often 1,500–2,300 mg per day depending on health status.

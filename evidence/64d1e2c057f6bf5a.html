I'm shopping for cottage cheese in the United States. I'm watching my sodium intake. Which brand or product should I choose, and why?

Reviewed answer

Your best bet is a “no salt added” cottage cheese. Regular cottage cheese is surprisingly salty: a half-cup serving generally contains around 300 to 400 milligrams of sodium, and some brands have much more. No-salt-added versions cut that by roughly 85–90%. The sodium that remains comes naturally from the milk. Top picks: Friendship Dairies Low Fat No Added Salt: This is the lowest I found. It has 45 milligrams of sodium per half cup, with 16 grams of protein and 1.5 grams of fat. Hood No Salt Added Low Fat: About 60 milligrams of sodium per half cup, along with 14 grams of protein and less than 1 gram of fat. EatingWell’s dietitians named it their best no-salt-added cottage cheese. It’s also fairly widely available. Store brands: Some supermarket chains make their own. For example, the Stop & Shop No Salt Added 1% version has 45 mg sodium and 16 g protein per half cup. Check whether your grocery store has an equivalent. A middle-ground option: If the unsalted taste is too bland for you, Breakstone’s makes a lower-sodium version. It has 200 milligrams of sodium per half-cup serving. That’s not as low as no-salt-added, but it’s still well below typical brands. Label tips: What the terms mean: “Reduced sodium” only means 25% less than the regular version, while “low sodium” means under 140 milligrams per serving. “No salt added” is usually the lowest of all. Low-fat isn’t automatically lower in sodium. Low-fat versions often have extra salt to make up for lost flavor. Always check the Nutrition Facts panel rather than assuming. Check the label at the store. Recipes change, so confirm the numbers on the tub in front of you. Unsalted cottage cheese tastes noticeably milder. Many people like it topped with fruit, cinnamon, black pepper, or fresh herbs, which add flavor without adding sodium.
